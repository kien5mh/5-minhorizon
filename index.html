Middle East

Iran says three tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz; the claim is unconfirmed

Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said on Monday, 23 Nov, that three oil tankers suffered explosions and fires after striking naval mines, and said they were under US air escort at the time. The Joint Maritime Information Centre had described earlier mine claims as unconfirmed, and US Central Command has called previous claims false; a UK maritime agency reported attacks on three tankers on Monday. Qatar's prime minister is pressing Washington to restart indirect talks, while the US Treasury announced sanctions on more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses and front companies. Shipping risk in the strait feeds directly into oil prices.

Europe

Russia threatens Estonia as Tallinn prepares to close all border crossings on 1 December

Estonia's government decided to suspend all border crossings with Russia for six months from 1 Dec, after accusing Russian military intelligence (GRU) of ordering a sabotage attack on a defense company. On Monday, 23 Nov, Moscow summoned Estonia's top diplomat and said Tallinn's actions were on the verge of triggering open war, claiming Ukrainian drones use Estonian airspace. Estonia's foreign ministry rejected the airspace claim, and NATO's Mark Rutte said the alliance is watching closely. Russian statements are unverified claims.

Source: ERR News; Al Jazeera ↗
Pacific

Australia and the Solomon Islands sign a security treaty as Honiara moves away from China

Prime Ministers Anthony Albanese and Matthew Wale signed a treaty in Canberra on Wednesday, 25 Nov, making Australia the Pacific nation's primary security partner and boosting support for its police force. ABC reported a day earlier that Honiara had told Beijing it was scrapping its 2022 security pact with China, but Wale declined to confirm that when asked on Wednesday (reports differ). The deal is a geopolitical setback for China in a region where Canberra and Beijing compete for influence.

Source: ABC News (Australia) ↗
South Asia

Sheikh Hasina says she will return to Bangladesh on 16 December; Dhaka says only extradition applies

The ousted former prime minister, who was sentenced to death in absentia for crimes against humanity over the 2024 protest crackdown, plans to return from exile in India on 16 Dec, her spokesperson said on Tuesday, 24 Nov. Bangladesh's government says she can come back only through extradition, and the chief prosecutor of the International Crimes Tribunal warned she faces execution if she returns. India has not handed her over. The standoff raises political and security risks in a major garment-exporting economy.

Source: Al Jazeera ↗
Southeast Asia

Indonesian candy factory fire kills 26 workers; police investigate possible negligence

Authorities confirmed 26 deaths from a fire that began on Saturday, 21 Nov, at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia plant in Serang, Banten province, with eight workers still missing. Rescuers said most victims were found on the second floor near the route to an emergency exit; about 300 people were inside and 29 were rescued. The company says its hydrants and exits were working, and police have opened a negligence investigation. The case is likely to renew scrutiny of workplace safety in Indonesian manufacturing.

Source: Associated Press (via ABC News) ↗
M&A

Sanofi agrees to buy US drugmaker Insmed for $41 billion

The French company announced the deal on Tuesday, 24 Nov, and its shares rose 9.7% to lead the STOXX Europe 600. European health-care stocks gained nearly 2% as investors rotated toward defensive sectors while technology shares fell back. The transaction still needs regulatory approvals, and its closing timetable was not in the report reviewed.

Source: Reuters (via Yahoo Finance) ↗
IPO

Anthropic closes below its $83 IPO price five days after its Nasdaq debut

The stock fell 6.5% on Tuesday, 24 Nov, to end under the offer price, after opening at $111.50 and closing at $97.17 on its 19 Nov debut. Reports tie the slide to the wider tech retreat, and TheStreet reported that the co-founders sold shares in the offering, which raised about $93.5 billion once underwriters used their extra-share option. Several brokers initiated coverage with buy-type ratings, including Canaccord Genuity (target $135) and Oppenheimer (target $150). Energy

Oil rebounds more than 2% as reports of Hormuz ship attacks outweigh a surplus outlook

Brent settled at $91.40 a barrel (+2.27%) and US crude at $82.05 (+2.35%) on 24 Nov, after both touched their lowest levels since August earlier in the session (Brent low: $88.10). A preliminary Reuters survey pointed to a rise in US crude and fuel stocks, and an analyst quoted said strategic reserve releases are keeping supply comfortable. Futures slipped about 0.5% in Asian trade on 25 Nov.

Central banks

New York Fed's Williams keeps a December rate hike in play with inflation near 3.5%

John Williams said on Monday, 23 Nov, that inflation is too high and the Fed still has work to do, leaving the door open to a hike at the 8-9 Dec meeting from the current 3.75%-4.00% range. He expects inflation to end 2026 at 3.5% and return to 2% only in 2028, and reporters were told he would not rule out back-to-back hikes. Markets price a quarter-point increase as near-certain, with a small chance of a half-point move. October PCE inflation data is due on Wednesday, 25 Nov.

Source: InvestingLive ↗
Commodities

Gold slides to about $3,550 on Fed hike bets, heading for a weak year

Spot gold fell about 2% on 24 Nov and was below $3,550 in Asian trade on 25 Nov, as expectations of more Fed rate rises weighed on the non-yielding metal even though the dollar and Treasury yields eased. Commerzbank said gold is likely to post its sharpest annual decline in 13 years unless it recovers soon. Trading Economics data show gold down about 11.6% over the past month. Traders are waiting for the PCE inflation reading.

Source: FXStreet; Trading Economics ↗
AI chips

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after President Trump presses the board

The board reversed course on Monday, 23 Nov, less than a week after its chair said Huang would not return following his resignation over revelations of extramarital affairs, one reportedly involving a woman suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence. Nvidia said US government input left it confident that his return poses no national security risk, and shares rose more than 30% that day, per CNN. Governance experts question the board's independence while its own investigation is unfinished, Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer criticized the move, and Reuters reported his exit had put some China orders at risk.

Source: Reuters (via Investing.com); CNN ↗
AI chips

Amazon agrees a reported $7.5 billion deal for Decart's AI optimization technology

Amazon announced on Monday, 23 Nov, that it will acquire Decart's optimization software and bring its founders and researchers into the company. The aim is to make Amazon's own Trainium chips more efficient as it prepares to sell them to customers as an alternative to Nvidia. Amazon did not disclose a price, and the $7.5 billion figure comes from press reports.

Source: TechCrunch ↗
Space

China launches Shenzhou-24 with Pakistan's first astronaut, the first foreign visitor to Tiangong

The crewed spacecraft lifted off from Jiuquan on the morning of Wednesday, 25 Nov, carrying two Chinese astronauts and Pakistani payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali. He is the first foreign national to board China's Tiangong space station and is expected to return after about a week with part of the departing crew. The mission extends China's space cooperation with partner countries.

Trade

The US is reported to be preparing Section 301 tariffs on China, the EU and 14 other economies

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer was expected to announce the measures as early as Tuesday evening, 24 Nov, based on an investigation into alleged excess industrial capacity; the goal is to rebuild tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February. Bloomberg reported that rates would not exceed those in existing trade deals and would be set economy by economy, while the Wall Street Journal reported that China, Vietnam and India could face higher rates and that the EU, Japan and South Korea would face 15% or less. At compile time no final announcement was confirmed (figures to be verified).

Source: Axios; Bloomberg; WSJ (as reported) ↗
Sanctions

The European Commission proposes a 22nd sanctions package against Russia

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced on Monday, 23 Nov, a package aimed at banks, energy and sanction evasion, including more than 70 additional shadow-fleet vessels and a ban on tankers insured in Russia. It would also bar EU firms from repairing Russian refineries, create an EU visa ban for Russian ex-combatants, and restrict Russian symbols at sporting events. All 27 member states must approve it before it takes effect.

Source: Brussels Times; Xinhua ↗
Export controls

US adds Megaspeed and Alibaba's Apex unit to its trade blacklist over chip diversion risk

The Commerce Department listed Singapore-based Megaspeed and six related companies, including Alibaba's Apex Enterprise Solutions, citing a significant risk that advanced computing chips could be diverted to unauthorized users. Alibaba called the listing erroneous and said Apex never diverted restricted chips; Reuters reported Apex prepaid about $4.2 billion to rent capacity in Southeast Asia. The listing follows tighter US rules last week aimed at Chinese remote access to advanced AI chips via Southeast Asia, and it does not by itself prove any illegal transfer.

Source: Reuters (via RTE) ↗

She told ICC President Tomoko Akane of the decision at a meeting in Tokyo. The move shows how states are adjusting to the threat of US sanctions on the tribunal (single source).
